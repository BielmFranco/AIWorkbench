# Task flow and evidence

## Frame the task
Describe what the user is trying to achieve, their starting context, decisions, failure consequences and current workaround. Identify which information is observed and which is a hypothesis. Do not invent interviews, participant counts or quotes.

## Include recovery
A workspace-connection flow includes account selection, permission request, pending feedback, denied permission and recovery. Explain consequences close to the decision. Keep the user's intent available when retrying and avoid demanding unrelated information.

## Test the risky assumption
Define a task and a success observation before a study. For example, 'connect the correct workspace and explain the requested permission' is more informative than asking whether participants like a button. Record hesitation, wrong turns, recovery and completion separately.

Use supplied research without silently enlarging it. A prototype critique can identify likely friction but is not a usability study. Contacting participants, sending messages or accessing external research requires authorization for those actions.
