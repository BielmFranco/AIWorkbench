# Visual direction grounded in the brief

## Make a consequential choice
Choose a layout, type treatment, palette and imagery approach that fits the audience and content. Explain the role of the main visual decision. A repeated card grid or decorative gradient can be appropriate when the brief benefits from it; do not prohibit a style just because it is common.

## Plan and inspect
Before building, map the primary action and reading order using real headings, labels and values. Specify type roles, color roles and spacing relationships. Then inspect the rendered result with the longest labels, dense and sparse content, and narrow screens. Revise the observed hierarchy or legibility problem before adding ornament.

## Critique anchors
An acceptable screen preserves readable information and the primary task in its relevant states. A strong screen also gives the content a coherent, distinctive expression. Record concrete evidence such as clipped plan limits or competing actions rather than claiming that a screen is 'premium'.

If screenshots are unavailable, deliver a clearly labeled direction or mockup and describe the checks still needed. Do not claim visual QA from markup alone. Accessibility and interaction behavior remain product requirements, including supplied reduced-motion preferences.
